## Design Context

### Users
- **Primary**: University department heads, deans, program coordinators evaluating curriculum licensing
- **Secondary**: Private school administrators, independent facilitators, professors
- **Context**: They're evaluating whether to adopt (and pay for) a case-study platform for AI governance education. They need to trust this is serious, not a weekend project.
- **Job to be done**: Quickly assess credibility, understand what they'd get, and take a next step (download sample or book a pilot)

### Brand Personality
**Credible, thoughtful, cutting-edge**
- Feels like a well-funded think tank or university press, not a SaaS startup
- Serious but not stuffy — modern institutional
- Should signal academic rigor + contemporary design awareness
- Anti-reference: generic EdTech landing pages with stock photos and neon gradients

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Tone**: Editorial-institutional, clean, restrained, warm
- **Reference**: veritasai.com — narrow palette, generous whitespace, photography-driven, restraint signals confidence, institutional credibility without being stuffy
- **Theme**: Light mode only
- **Palette**: Warm cream (#FAF9F7), deep navy (#1A1A2E), terracotta accent (#9B3A2B), slate muted (#64748B)
- **Typography**: Source Serif 4 (headings), Inter (body), IBM Plex Mono (labels/eyebrows)

### Design Principles
1. **Restraint signals credibility** — fewer elements, more whitespace, narrower palette. Every visual choice should feel deliberate.
2. **Content does the selling** — the scenarios and facilitator materials are the product. Let them speak. Don't decorate over substance.
3. **Institutional feel, not startup feel** — this goes in front of procurement committees. It should feel like a publisher or research institution, not a Y Combinator demo day.
4. **Consistency builds trust** — same patterns, same components, same spacing. Visual drift erodes the perception of quality.
5. **Every detail matters at this stage** — prospective customers are evaluating quality from the website. Sloppy details = sloppy product.
